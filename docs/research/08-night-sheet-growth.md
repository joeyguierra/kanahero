# Research 08 — how the night sheet should grow: spacing, mixing and size for grammar (2026-11-26)

Question (learner, 2026-11-26): the night sheet is fixed at 3 · 4 · 4 · 5 = 16 items. Should it grow
as words and missed points pile up, and by what rule? Asked for research before any rule.
Nothing is added before Fri 11-27 (fully timed week); a rule, if any, starts Sun 11-29.

Confidence legend as `00-survey.md`. Abstract/summary level unless marked. Most grammar studies are
EFL classroom or lab, a few sessions, adults; none is a single adult self-studying N5 Japanese.

## Findings

1. **Spacing works for grammar, not only words (A−).** Bird 2010 (longitudinal, L2 English syntax):
   distributed practice beat massed on long-term tests. Kim & Webb 2022 meta-analysis (37 studies)
   confirms spacing in L2 overall, with effects varying by area, learner and activity (via Serrano
   2022 review). → Points that appear once and never return will fade; the sheet needs review.

2. **The exact gap matters little; short first gaps are defensible (B, mixed).** Grammar lag studies
   disagree: longer lags won in Bird 2010 and Rogers 2015; shorter in Suzuki 2017 and Suzuki &
   DeKeyser 2017 (Serfaty & Serrano 2022). Suzuki 2017 (Language Learning, morphology, 4 sessions):
   3.3-day spacing beat 7-day from session 3 on, held at 7- and 28-day posttests, small–medium
   effects. Kasprowicz et al. 2019 (children, French): 3.5- vs 7-day, minimal difference. Matches
   `research/02` finding 4 (interval shape has no clear winner). → Reuse the check's ladder
   (miss → next day; ✓ → 3 → 7 → 14) rather than tune a new one.

3. **Mixing grammar exercises helps retention (B).** Nakata & Suzuki 2019 (Modern Language Journal,
   Japanese EFL learners): mixing (interleaving) grammar exercises improved long-term retention over
   blocking. Pan et al. 2024: interleaving improved learning of similar and dissimilar tenses
   (Romance languages). Suzuki et al. 2020 (relative clauses, speaking): interleaved more accurate on
   immediate and delayed tests.

4. **Mix the confusable ones together (A for the moderator).** Brunmair & Richter 2019 meta-analysis
   (59 studies): overall interleaving effect moderate (g ≈ 0.42, high heterogeneity); stronger when
   categories are *similar to each other*, less similar within, and material is complex. → The pairs
   this learner actually mixes up are the ones to put side by side: に / の / で · この / その / あの ·
   ます / ました / ません / ませんでした · drop-る vs り-verbs (いる, つける vs のる, かえる).
   Caution kept from `00-survey.md` #4: interleaving similar *new words* can hurt; this is for
   rules/categories already introduced.

5. **Hybrid beats either pure schedule (B).** Suzuki & Sunada 2019 (SSLA): blocked practice followed
   by interleaved ("hybrid") developed the skill more than blocked or interleaved alone; and learners
   tested on the skill they practised beat those tested on the other skill (comprehension vs
   production). → Keep the new point blocked (A–C), then mix it with old points; keep both directions
   (meaning and production), since each mostly trains itself (`research/02` finding 1).

6. **Retrieval, one pass per item (A).** Classroom quizzing ≈ g 0.50 (Yang et al. 2021 meta-analysis,
   via secondary source); retrieval-practice benefits extend to rule-based learning and far transfer
   (2024, artificial language). One retrieval per item per session is the time-efficient dose
   (Nakata 2016, `research/02` finding 5). → Each due point gets 1–2 items in review, not a block.

7. **Learners left alone block, not mix (B).** Lu, Penney & Kang 2020: given the choice, learners
   overwhelmingly block. → The rule picks what returns, not the learner's sense of what is weak
   (consistent with `00-survey.md` #3).

## What this gives the night sheet (proposal, for `decisions.md` once agreed)

- **New point stays blocked:** A 3 · B 4 · C 4, one Minna point a night (finding 5).
- **E — review, mixed:** every earlier point (night sheets + morning items) carries its own schedule
  from its own results: ✗ → next night · ✓ → 3 → 7 → 14 days (findings 1–2). Each due point gives
  **1 item, 2 if it was missed**; directions alternate meaning / production (5); items from
  confusable points sit next to each other and next to tonight's point (3–4). E has no fixed size.
- **D — forms, mixed across sets:** next week's set (て-form) is drilled with still-missed earlier
  forms mixed in (いる → wasn't, つける), not replacing them (3–4).
- **Morning items:** 2 night's point + 1 form + **1 review** (oldest due point).
- **Size is the learner's call:** never trimmed unless he says so; agent flags a night over ~25 min.

## Falsified by
- Morning **review** items (points last seen ≥ 3 days earlier) under ~70% across week 3 → spacing
  too wide or review too thin.
- Sealed particle / forms checks not up on or after 2026-12-21 (existing falsifiers).
- Night time rising week on week while morning scores stay flat → growth is copying, not learning.
